No matter the size of your team, some amount of meetings are necessary to get work done. The problem is that most people don’t actually like their 
[Meeting Operating System](https://www.lucidmeetings.com/glossary/meeting-operating-system#:~:text=A%20meeting%20operating%20system%20establishes,five%20levels%20of%20performance%20maturity.)

. Have you ever wondered how much time your team spends in meetings? Or which of them has the best technique for managing free time? Or wanted to take a hacksaw to a particularly ineffective meeting?

Over the years, I’ve become a deep student of how teams run meetings. In my time leading YouTube, we experimented and developed our 
[own unique meeting OS](https://coda.io/@shishir/rituals-for-hypergrowth-an-inside-look-at-how-youtube-scaled)

. Since starting Coda, I’ve had a chance to work with thousands of companies and observe their best practices. In a discussion with my teammates Justin and Erin, we talked about whether we could build a process for teams to audit and self-discover their own best practices. That brainstorm led to this doc. 
## A systematic approach to understand (and fix) your meetings

We have collected our thoughts from dozens of company interviews into a single system. This doc is meant as an 
active toolkit
 for a team to collectively audit and improve their meeting systems. This is not just theory — we’re going to help you put it in practice.

The mechanics are simple: this doc syncs you and your team’s calendar data and takes you through a set of simple surveys to debug your Meeting Operating System in ~30-60 minutes (and can be done asynchronously as well). It’s most useful when done as a team, but the exercises actually work for individuals too. 
### 3 key tests to audit your team’s meetings

The audit is intended for a team that works closely together. The doc will lead your team through analysis and discussion:

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/new-moon.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/new-moon.svg)

1. Balance test](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/1-balance-test-44)

 

How much time does the team spend in meetings versus free time? 

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/cabbage.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/cabbage.svg)

2. Flow test](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/2-flow-test-57)

 

Not all free time is created equal. Paul Graham wrote a wonderful piece on the value of uninterrupted time in 
[Maker’s Schedule, Manager’s Schedule](http://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html)

. How much of your free time is uninterrupted?

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/doughnut-chart.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/doughnut-chart.svg)

3. Effectiveness test](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/3-effectiveness-test-23)

 

Spending time in meetings is not necessarily a bad thing… as long as they are effective! How effective does your team feel your meetings are?
### 4 steps to improve your Meeting Operating System

With the insights from the 
[audit summary](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/4-audit-summary-59)

 in hand, you use these four steps to shape your Meeting Operating System and improve your meetings:

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/week-view.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/week-view.svg)

1. Define our current Meeting OS](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/1-define-our-current-meeting-os-61)


Let’s start by baselining – what’s your current meeting OS? For many teams, a meeting OS can be as simple as just a list of the team’s shared meetings. By documenting and categorizing this list, your team can adapt to drive the alignment and values you aspire to.

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/do-not-mix.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/do-not-mix.svg)

2. Minimize ad-hoc meetings](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/2-minimize-ad-hoc-meetings-62)


Ad-hoc meetings require schedule coordination and can have calendar impacts that ripple throughout the team. And more important, they lack the clear structure necessary to be productive. By minimizing ad-hoc meetings, teams can create the 
right
, regular forums with enough time and the appropriate attendees. 

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/dj.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/dj.svg)

3. Find flow](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/3-find-flow-64)

 

It’s worth repeating: Not all free time is created equal. This step uncovers flow disruptors — any meeting where the time before or after is less than your designated flow time threshold. Let’s find flow!

[![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/ruby-gemstone.svg)![](https://cdn.coda.io/icons/svg/color/ruby-gemstone.svg)

4. Drive effectiveness](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/4-drive-effectiveness-63)

 

Now that we know which meetings we have, and how they fit together — how can we make them more effective? 

Each step will show us new data, lead us through a brainstorming exercise, and culminate in an 
[Action Plan](https://coda.io/@shishir/team-meeting-audit/5-action-plan-for-our-meeting-os-65)

.
